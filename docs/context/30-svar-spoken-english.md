# Mock SVAR — spoken English assessment

## 1. What this is

Mock SVAR measures how well you use English — speaking, listening, reading, and vocabulary and
grammar. You read sentences aloud, repeat sentences you hear, speak freely on a topic, answer
listening questions, and answer vocabulary and grammar questions. It reports your level on the
internationally recognised **CEFR** scale, from A1 (beginner) to C2 (mastery).

It **adapts**. Each section tracks your level separately and moves up or down based on how you
perform, so the questions you get are pitched at your actual ability.

## 2. Who can use it

Every candidate, on both plans. It is **not** a PRO-only feature — Basic users get the full test
and a report. PRO changes only how many attempts you get per week and gives you the advanced
report.

## 4. How it works, step by step

1. Open **Mock SVAR** and start a test. You need a working microphone.
2. Work through five kinds of question:

| Section | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Read aloud** | A sentence appears; you read it out loud |
| **Repeat sentence** | You hear a sentence once and repeat it |
| **Free speech** | You are given a topic and speak about it |
| **Listening comprehension** | You hear a conversation and answer multiple-choice questions |
| **Vocabulary and grammar** | Multiple-choice questions on words, idioms and grammar |

3. Submit. Scoring happens in the background — speech has to be transcribed and analysed.
4. Your report appears under **Report**, with a level for each skill and a written summary.

Each test is **13 questions** in total, distributed across the five sections.

## 5. Rules, limits and timings

| Rule | Basic | PRO |
|---|---|---|
| Attempts per week | **3** | **4** |

- A microphone is required. Without it you cannot take the speaking sections.
- **You never see the same question twice.** Questions are drawn without repetition across all your
  attempts.
- Each section keeps its **own level**. You can be moving up in reading while holding steady in
  speaking — that is normal and intended.
- Once your weekly limit is reached you must wait for the week to roll over, or use an assessment
  credit from a top-up bundle.

## 6. How scoring works

**The six levels:**

| Level | CEFR | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | A1 | Beginner |
| L2 | A2 | Elementary |
| L3 | B1 | Intermediate |
| L4 | B2 | Upper intermediate |
| L5 | C1 | Advanced |
| L6 | C2 | Mastery |

**Your four skill scores** are built up from the sections:

| Skill | Built from |
|---|---|
| **Speaking** | Read aloud, repeat sentence, free speech |
| **Listening** | Repeat sentence, listening comprehension |
| **Reading** | Read aloud |
| **Vocabulary and grammar** | The vocabulary and grammar section |

**What is actually measured in your speech:**

- **Pronunciation** — how clearly each word comes across.
- **Fluency** — your pace, how many filler words ("um", "uh") you use, and how long your pauses
  are. There is a target speaking speed for each level; being far above or below it costs marks.
- **Accuracy** — for read-aloud and repeat-sentence, how closely what you said matches what you
  were meant to say.
- **For free speech**, additionally: coherence, range of vocabulary, grammatical accuracy, and how
  well you manage a longer stretch of speech.

Multiple-choice questions are simply right or wrong.

**How your level moves**, per section:

| Your performance at your current level | What happens |
|---|---|
| Below 30% | **Drops a level immediately** |
| 30–49% | Drops a level if you score in this band **twice in a row** |
| 50–84% | Stays where it is |
| 85%+ but you struggled with the harder questions | Stays where it is |
| 85%+ **and** you handled the harder questions well | Moves up a level after **two such results in a row** |

Each test mixes questions at your level with a few from the level above (or below, if you are
already at L6) — that is how it checks whether you are ready to move up.

## 7. What you get afterwards

- **A level for each of the four skills**, on the A1–C2 scale.
- **A progress score out of 100** per skill. Each level occupies about 17 points, so L3 sits
  between roughly 33 and 50. Moving from the bottom to the top of a level moves the score without
  changing the level — that is real progress, shown.
- **A written summary** of a few sentences explaining where you are and what to work on. If you
  have taken the test before, it includes how you have changed since last time.
- **Section-by-section scores.**

## 8. When things go wrong

### "My score is much lower than I expected"
The most common causes are microphone quality and speaking speed. A distant or noisy microphone
directly lowers pronunciation scores because the words come through less clearly. Speaking much
faster or slower than natural pace costs fluency marks. Use a headset in a quiet room.

### "I got dropped a level"
A single very weak section (below 30%) drops that section's level immediately, without waiting. It
is not a punishment — the next test will pitch questions where you can actually show what you know,
and you can move straight back up.

### "My level did not go up even though I scored well"
Two things are required: a strong score at your level **and** handling the harder questions mixed
into the test **and** doing both twice in a row. One excellent test is not enough by design.

### "My speaking level is high but reading is low" (or any mismatch)
Each section is tracked separately and they genuinely can diverge. Reading is measured only from
read-aloud, so it moves with your read-aloud performance specifically.

### "The recording did not capture my answer"
Check the microphone permission in your browser and that no other app (Zoom, Meet, Teams) is
holding the microphone. If a question recorded nothing, it scores as no answer.

### "I have run out of attempts"
You get 3 per week on Basic and 4 on PRO. Wait for the week to roll over, upgrade, or buy an
assessment bundle top-up.

### "My report is not ready"
Speech has to be transcribed and analysed, so SVAR reports take longer than multiple-choice ones.
Check the **Report** section; you get a notification when it is ready.

## 9. Common questions

**Do I need to pay for SVAR?**
No. It is available on the free plan. PRO gives you one extra attempt per week and the advanced
report.

**How long does it take?**
Thirteen questions across five sections. Free-speech answers are the longest part.

**Does my accent lower my score?**
No. It measures clarity, fluency, accuracy, vocabulary and grammar — not which accent you have.

**Can I skip a question?**
Skipping scores that question as unanswered, which lowers the section score.

**Can I retake it to get a better level?**
Yes, within your weekly limit. Levels move on consistent performance, not on one good attempt.

**What is a good level?**
B2 (L4) is generally the threshold for confident professional communication in English. B1 (L3) is
functional. The report tells you where you are and what is next.

**Why did I get an easier test this time?**
Your level dropped in that section, so the questions are pitched lower. Score well and it moves
back up.

**Can I see the correct answers?**
The report gives you scores, levels and a written summary rather than an answer key.

## 10. What this does not do

- It does not test writing.
- It does not give you a pass or fail — it gives you a level.
- It does not judge your accent.
- It does not let you repeat a question or re-record an answer.
- It does not show the same question twice across attempts.

## 11. When to contact support

- An attempt was consumed but no report appeared after a reasonable wait.
- Recordings consistently capturing nothing despite microphone permission being granted.
- A level that changed in a way the rules above do not explain.
