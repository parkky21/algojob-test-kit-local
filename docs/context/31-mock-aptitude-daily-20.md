# Mock Aptitude — the Daily 20

## 1. What this is

Twenty questions a day across four sections, designed to be taken regularly rather than crammed.
What makes it different from ordinary practice is the report: instead of just telling you how many
you got right, it tells you **what kind of mistake you made** — whether you did not know the
method, knew it but were too slow, or rushed and threw away a mark you had.

Each topic tracks your level separately, so the paper you get tomorrow is pitched at what you
showed today.

## 2. Who can use it

Every candidate, on both plans. It is not a PRO-only feature.

## 4. How it works, step by step

1. Open **Mock Aptitude** and start. It runs in a secure test window — see
   `10-web-app-and-test-environment.md` for what that involves.
2. Work through four sections, **five questions each**:

| Section | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Data Interpretation (DI)** | Bar charts, pie charts and tables — one chart with five questions on it |
| **Quantitative Aptitude** | Numerical and mathematical reasoning |
| **Logical Reasoning** | Patterns, deduction, arrangements |
| **English** | Grammar, vocabulary, comprehension |

3. **Each section has its own clock.** When a section's time runs out you move to the next one;
   unfinished questions in that section are recorded as unreached.
4. Submit at the end. Your report is ready almost immediately.

A typical paper takes about **21 minutes**. The exact time budget is calculated from the specific
questions you were given — harder questions get more time — so it varies with your level.

## 5. Rules, limits and timings

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Questions per paper | **20** (4 sections × 5) |
| Typical duration | about **21 minutes** |
| Timing | **per section**, not one clock for the whole paper |
| Attempts per week | **3** on Basic, **4** on PRO |
| Topic levels | 1 to 5, starting at **2** |

- **Topics rotate.** There are many topics per section and only five slots, so topics cycle through
  in turn. A topic you saw today will come back in a few days, not tomorrow.
- **Skipping is not free.** A question you skip while you still had time is recorded as a careless
  mistake, not as "did not know" — see scoring below.
- Once your weekly limit is reached, wait for the week to roll over or use an assessment credit.

## 6. How scoring works

**Your report does not lead with a score.** It classifies each of your twenty answers into one of
five categories, based on whether you got it right *and* how long you took relative to the time
that question was expected to need:

| Category | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Mastered** | Right, and within the expected time. Clean. |
| **Fragile** | Right, but slower than expected. You know it — the method is too slow. |
| **Careless** | Wrong, but answered very fast — **or skipped while you still had time**. You rushed, it was not a knowledge gap. |
| **Gap** | Wrong, with time properly spent. The method genuinely is not there yet. |
| **Unreached** | The section clock ran out before you got to it. No evidence either way. |

Two things surprise people, and both are deliberate:
- **A deliberate skip counts as careless, not as a gap.** You chose not to attempt something you
  had time for.
- **Being right but slow is called out as a problem.** In a real timed test, slow-but-correct does
  not survive. That is why "fragile" exists as its own category.

**Your topic level** moves on consistent evidence, not one result. A topic needs **two signals in
the same direction** to move up or down — a good result opens the possibility, and a second one
confirms it. A result in the opposite direction cancels it rather than flipping straight over. Levels
run 1 to 5 and everyone starts at 2.

- "Mastered" pushes a topic up. "Gap" pushes it down. "Fragile", "careless" and "unreached" move
  nothing — they are evidence about your **pacing**, not your level.
- Because topics rotate, a level change usually takes a few papers to complete. This is normal.

**Your section progress score (0–100)** combines the level you are working at with how well you are
doing at it. Each level is worth 20 points, so level 3 sits between 40 and 60. It reads **0 until
you have completed your first test.**

## 7. What you get afterwards

Six things, in this order:

1. **A headline** naming the main pattern — for example *"You know more than the clock is letting
   you show"* when you had several fragile answers, or *"You're losing marks to speed, not to
   knowledge"* for careless ones. It never leads with a number.
2. **Tiles** — how many of your twenty fell into each category.
3. **A section table** — right out of total, clock used against the budget, and one sentence on
   each section.
4. **Findings** — the actual patterns: a cluster of slow-but-right answers in one section, rushing
   while you still had time on the clock, or several wrong answers sharing the same underlying
   concept. If nothing stands out, it says so rather than inventing a pattern.
5. **Actions** — up to six specific things to do, ordered by priority. Gaps come first ("relearn X"
   with the explanation), then fragile answers (the faster method for that question type), then
   pacing.
6. **Question-by-question review** — each question with the explanation, and specifically why the
   option *you* picked was wrong.

For questions you got right and fast, the explanation is hidden — you did not need it.

## 8. When things go wrong

### "Why is my score 0 / why is my progress empty?"
The section progress score reads 0 until you have completed your first test. Complete one paper and
it populates.

### "I answered everything right but it says 'fragile'"
Fragile means correct but slower than the question was expected to take. It is telling you the
method works but will not survive a timed exam. The action list gives you the faster approach.

### "I skipped a question I did not know — why is that 'careless'?"
Because you had time left. Skipping with time in hand is classified as a pacing decision, not a
knowledge gap. If you genuinely do not know it, spending the time and getting it wrong is recorded
more usefully, as a gap.

### "I ran out of time and questions are marked 'unreached'"
That section's clock expired. Unreached questions are not counted against your knowledge — they are
recorded as no evidence. But a section full of unreached questions is itself the finding, and the
report will say so.

### "The same topics keep coming back"
Topics cycle in turn across papers. With many topics per section and five slots per paper, a given
topic comes round every few days.

### "My level did not change even though I did well"
A level needs two consistent results in the same direction. Because topics rotate, this typically
takes a few papers.

### "A data interpretation set felt too easy or too hard"
DI questions come as complete sets built around one chart, and the available sets do not span every
difficulty evenly yet. The level tracking still runs, but DI difficulty varies less than the other
sections.

### "The report suggests a shortcut for one question but not another"
Shortcuts are only suggested where one is on file for that specific question type. Where there is
none, the report stays quiet rather than inventing one.

## 9. Common questions

**How long does it take?**
About 21 minutes, varying with your level. Each section is timed separately.

**Is it really daily?**
The paper is designed as a daily habit, but your plan limit is **weekly** — 3 per week on Basic, 4
on PRO.

**Can I pause it?**
No. Each section runs on its own clock.

**Do I get marks for attempting?**
No negative marking and no attempt marks. What matters is the category each answer falls into.

**Why does it not just show my score out of 20?**
The section table shows right-out-of-total for each section. The report leads with the pattern
because "12/20" does not tell you what to fix and "you know it but you are too slow in quant" does.

**Does tomorrow's paper focus on my weak topics?**
No. Topics rotate in turn, evenly. The report tells you what to work on; it does not re-weight the
next paper.

**What happened to MCQ practice?**
It now runs on this engine. If you go to the old practice page you will be sent here.

**Can I review past reports?**
Yes, under **Report**. Your progress chart tracks each section over time.

## 10. What this does not do

- It does not over-sample your weak topics in the next paper — topic rotation is even.
- It does not apply negative marking.
- It does not let you pause, or return to a section once its clock has expired.
- It does not give you a single overall score out of 20 as the headline.

## 11. When to contact support

- A completed paper that produced no report.
- A section clock that expired far sooner than the displayed budget.
- An attempt consumed without the test loading.
